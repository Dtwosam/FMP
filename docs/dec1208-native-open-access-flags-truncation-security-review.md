**Critical conclusion:** A read-only descriptor's inability to perform a later `pwrite`, and the fact that its eventual close is labeled **IN_CLOSE_NOWRITE**, do **not establish** that no source data were already modified. In the observed Linux O_RDONLY|O_TRUNC case, **the open itself destroyed the file's contents**, before any explicit data-write syscall and while the later descriptor was read-only. Conversely, the O_PATH special path-only mode **ignores O_TRUNC** on the probed kernel and does not generate open/read/modify events. O_TRUNC and O_RDONLY flags must be understood together, and behavior must be validated on the actual target platform, not assumed globally. The distinction is particularly important for any claim that userspace opens are independently OS-enforced read-only.
